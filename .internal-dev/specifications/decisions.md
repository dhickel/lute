# Durable Decisions

## Main menu and local results

- **Decision:** Normal startup opens the main menu. Story is unavailable; Hoard requires a nonblank player name of up to 16 letters, numbers, spaces, dashes, or underscores. On completion, results show score, kills, round, survival time, and a locally stored top-five score board, with replay and main-menu choices. Earlier saved runs win score ties.
- **Options and input:** The menu provides local persisted overrides for the four debug layers and keyboard and controller bindings. Conflicting bindings require confirmation; mouse bindings remain authored in input settings. Keyboard, controller, and mouse support menu navigation. Preferences are local, not shared or online.
- **Startup and assets:** The generator requires `raw_assets/images/menu/mainmenubg.jpg` and publishes it to `assets/images/menu/mainmenubg.jpg`. The JVM property `-DluteDirectMap=true` opts into loading the configured startup map directly; the Gradle run task forwards `-PluteDirectMap=true`. Direct-map startup bypasses the menu and its local overrides.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Local preferences provide a lightweight menu and leaderboard without an online service; direct-map startup remains available for development and tests.
- **Affected specifications:** Input settings below; `README.md` and `docs/input-system.md` describe usage.
- **Source:** Main menu implementation on the current branch.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if modes, persistence, or startup routing change.

## Hoard sprint prototype

- **Decision:** On `hoard-mode-alpha`, holding controller B while moving ramps the Hoard player up to 1.6× normal speed over 250 ms. On release or exhaustion, the boost ramps down to walking speed in up to 100 ms. Full stamina permits 2 seconds of boosted movement at the existing drain rate. Recharge begins after 2 active simulation seconds without actual stamina expenditure; pauses freeze this delay. The recharge rate remains 70% of the original rate, taking about 8.57 seconds to refill once recharge begins, or about 10.57 seconds from the last drain when empty. The walk animation follows the boost up to 2× speed while advancing. Stamina drains only when the physics step makes actual progress in the movement direction, including during the release tail while stamina remains. Once exhausted, the player must release and press B again to run as stamina refills.
- **Presentation and scope:** The Hoard HUD places health and stamina in a centered row below the arena stats. Sprint and this HUD layout are Hoard-only. This prototype adds no backpedaling, dash, invincibility frames, or other gameplay changes; existing dash prototypes remain on separate branches.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** A hold-to-run stamina meter provides bounded speed without charging for blocked movement. Requiring a fresh press after exhaustion prevents automatic resumption from the same hold.
- **Affected specifications:** The Hoard arena run decision below and its HUD and movement behavior on `hoard-mode-alpha`.
- **Source:** User-directed sprint prototype implemented on `hoard-mode-sprint-test`, based on `hoard-mode-alpha`.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if the prototype's movement or stamina rules change.

## Hoard arena run

- **Decision:** The map ID `hoard_mode` starts a map-scoped arena run using the required enemy polygons `SpawnArea1` through `SpawnArea4`. The player starts at 1,000 health with a maximum of 1,000. Death is terminal for the arena visit; ordinary-map respawn is unchanged.
- **Health pickups:** Round-start healing is replaced by one 250 HP pickup at a random position inside the center health ellipse for each completed round, clear of Dredge's spawn point. Each individual player-credited enemy kill independently has a 10% chance to drop a 50 HP pickup. Healing cannot raise health above the maximum. Successful pickup healing displays the actual HP gained as green floating text in the form `+N HP`, at twice the damage-number size; it rises and fades. Ineffective healing produces no text.
- **Rounds and waves:** Each round lasts 120 simulation seconds. All four areas are active from round 1 and draw from the same shared roster, with each unlocked archer type (`undead_archer`, `cultist_archer`, and `viper`) weighted half as much as each melee type. The roster starts with `brainless_undead` and `undead_archer`, then adds `bone_soldier` in round 5, `cultist` and `cultist_archer` in round 10, `viper` in round 20, `reaper` in round 25, and `raider` in round 30. Dredge spawns separately every fifth round indefinitely. The first batch of a fresh visit is exactly 10 enemies total across all zones. Each later round opens with a batch based on 10; timed refreshes use a random base of 4–8 inclusive at random intervals of 30–50 simulation seconds inclusive. Both batch bases are multiplied by `1 + 0.05 × round` and rounded to the nearest integer, with ties rounded up. A newly entered run resets progression.
- **Dredge and weapons:** Dredge appears at rounds 5, 10, and every fifth round thereafter at the existing spawn. Dredge does not wander: it detects a living player in its full detection circle with clear feet-to-feet sight and pursues even outside the health ellipse. In normal Hoard play, the guard can commit to a lunge when the player is visible within 80 logical pixels. After about 0.16 seconds of planted pre-hit playback, half the previous approximately 0.33 seconds, it eases forward in fixed steps during the live hitbox frames without tracking the player; solid cover blocks damage. When detection is lost, it normally returns to its spawn and waits, reacquiring the player on the way if visible. A damaging player arrow provokes pursuit beyond normal sight and range until solid body contact or the player is no longer live, then normal detection and combat rules resume; this adds no pathfinding. Hoard spawns multiply Dredge's authored base health by `1 + 0.05 × round` and round to the nearest integer, with ties rounded up. With the local authored base of 300 HP, that is 375 in round 5 and 450 in round 10. Its random weapon bag excludes `basic_bow` and `basic_arrow`. The player starts each Hoard visit with the basic sword and basic bow in an unlimited inventory of distinct weapons, without changing the equipped weapon. R and controller Y cycle that inventory, and acquiring a weapon does not automatically equip it.
- **Hammer exception and demo:** In Hoard, the player's `basic_hammer` and `black_hammer` secondary attacks are heavy lunges using the primary animation and hitbox, with 25% more damage and 0.2 seconds added cooldown. They retain the secondary slot; primary attacks are unchanged. Player and Dredge hammer lunges halve the planted pre-hit playback from about 0.33 to about 0.16 seconds, then drive in fixed steps with a moving sensor throughout the existing hitbox window. Both travel 48 logical pixels unobstructed, and the hitbox on the final fixed movement tick is enlarged 1.5× in linear dimensions. Each lunge damages a target at most once per swing, and cover blocks damage. Dredge carries contacted players and enemies a bounded distance in the lunge direction. The main-menu Hammer Demo uses a separate unscored map runtime over the same arena art, without regular Hoard round logic or Dredge. It uses standard Hoard stamina and sprint, HP, and kill-healing chance; the player starts with `basic_axe` equipped and all weapons available. The first batch of 10 undead spawns at 15 simulation seconds, with another batch every 15 simulation seconds. Spawns use clear positions in the central zone and target the player. The demo forces enemy infighting chance to 0 regardless of the normal setting, disabling enemy retaliation targeting without changing collateral damage. A fresh demo instance resets the timer. Scored Hoard remains unchanged. These are runtime arena exceptions, with no data schema changes; behavior outside Hoard and the demo is unchanged.
- **Score and lifecycle:** Each credited player kill of a map-owned enemy adds one kill and awards XP to the existing run Score. The base XP is 5 for the round 1 roster, 10 for the round 5 unlock, 15 for round 10, 20 for round 20, 25 for round 25, and 30 for round 30; Dredge has a separate base of 100. Multiply the base by `1 + 0.05 × round` using the current round at kill time, then round to the nearest integer with ties rounded up. Night does not affect score, and earlier kills are not repriced when the round changes. The final result shows score, round, and active survival time. Hoard has permanent daylight, N does not toggle its lighting, and its HUD has no time-of-day display. The session world clock freezes during Hoard visits and resumes outside; it does not drive the round countdown or active survival time. A new session starts at 06:00, map travel does not reset the clock, and pause and screen lifecycle apply to the clock and rounds. A map binding starts a new run rather than preserving arena counters. Non-Hoard behavior remains unchanged.
- **Asset preflight:** Required settings boolean `debugLayers.assetValidation` is `true` in shipped settings and exposed as **Asset Validation** in Options. When enabled, Hoard preparation checks delayed encounter dependencies before installing the arena, including Dredge, both health pickups, the weapon bag, starter weapons and their required assets and behaviors. When false, only these extra delayed-content checks are skipped; ordinary loads and validation still apply. Explicit visual launch overrides do not affect Asset Validation. Valid gameplay is unchanged.
- **Object behavior:** Object behavior fields, including pickup, animation, and emission, are optional and independent so an object can combine the behaviors it needs.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** A map-scoped run reuses existing combat and placement without introducing persistence. Pickups replace automatic round healing, while permanent daylight and the frozen session clock leave the round countdown and active survival time independent of world time.
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md); object authoring is documented in `docs/asset-service.md`.
- **Source:** User-accepted Hoard arena behavior.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if arena lifecycle or rules change.

## Lighting debug views

- **Decision:** Remove the F3 debug-cycle binding and action. Required `debugLayers` settings independently select four JSON booleans: `physics`, `triggers`, `rawLightMap`, and `lightingOutlines`. The local sample sets the first two true and the latter two false; these are authored values, not fallback defaults. Missing, extra, or invalid members fail settings loading. Edit `raw_assets/game.settings.json`, regenerate runtime assets, and restart to apply changes. N's night toggle remains available outside the hoard arena.
- **Rendering and tradeoff:** Layers can run concurrently. At night `rawLightMap` replaces world art with the ambient-plus-emitter lightmap; by day it leaves the ordinary world view unchanged. `lightingOutlines` draws orange outlines for active non-sensor Box2D fixtures and green rings at current pulsed emitter radii by day or night. Physics, trigger, lighting, and aim overlays can appear over the raw lightmap. Fixture tracing is shared with sensor debug rendering; diagnostics neither alter physics nor toggle night. Collision geometry may differ from visible art.
- **Developer launch:** The shared IntelliJ `.run` configurations `Lute Run` and `Lute Debug Run` call `:lwjgl3:run` with `-PluteDebugLayers=off` and `-PluteDebugLayers=on`, respectively. The launch profile establishes visual defaults: `off` disables all overlays, while `on` enables physics and trigger overlays and leaves the special lighting views off. Without the flag, authored `debugLayers` settings provide the defaults. In menu-started gameplay, explicit persisted Options overrides take precedence over those defaults; direct-map startup bypasses those overrides and follows the launch profile. The raw light map is displayed only at night. Launch profiles do not alter asset JSON. Use the IDE Run button to enable game diagnostics through `Lute Debug Run`; the IDE Debug button is separate and is used for breakpoints.
- **Justification:** Independent authored layers permit simultaneous inspection of computed lighting and collision geometry without changing the simulation. Shared launch overrides make common diagnostics available without editing or regenerating assets; changing authored selections still requires regeneration and restart.
- **Affected specifications:** Session night lighting and input settings below; `docs/input-system.md` describes settings and lifecycle.
- **Source:** Current lighting debug implementation and its input, lifecycle, and real-GL regressions.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if debug controls or lighting diagnostics change.

## Session night lighting

- **Decision:** A new `GameScreen` starts in day. The tracked N binding toggles night on fresh active-context presses, independently of the player and world clock. Mode and pulse phase survive travel and hide/show; phase freezes while hidden or paused. Ambient is captured from required `nightAmbient` settings at startup, so changes require regeneration and restart.
- **Authoring:** Every shared OBJECT/ITEM definition requires explicitly nullable `animation`, `collision`, and `emissive`. A nonnull emitter requires color, positive pixel radius, intensity in (0, 1], and a required nullable pulse with positive period and independent radius and intensity strengths in [0, 1). The local campfire uses `#FFAA55`, 128 pixels, 0.9 intensity, and a 1.2-second pulse with strengths 0.12 and 0.18. Local ambient is `#8299CC` at 0.35 brightness. These are authored values, not fallbacks; FireSpawn remains in place.
- **Rendering and tradeoff:** Night modulates world art using ambient and independent radial lights. Active solid Box2D fixtures cast shadows regardless of collision filters; sensors and the emitter's own body do not. Another filled solid containing or touching the origin suppresses that light, while edges and chains remain unfilled. Filled collision outlines remain dark through the exit surface; behind it the shadow fades over the minimum of 0.75 times blocker size, 24 authored pixels, and 0.25 times the current pulsed light radius. Blocker size is the maximum vertex chord for polygons, diameter for circles, and segment length for edges and individual chain segments. Overlapping shadows retain the darkest visibility, independently for each light. Shadows reflect the last physics geometry rather than visual silhouettes; overlays and viewport bars are not darkened. Day avoids the lighting pass. No new assets, settings, or physics behavior are introduced for short shadows. Outside the hoard arena, automatic cycles, moon, and persistence are not part of this decision; the arena remains in daylight as specified above.
- **Justification and alternatives:** Collision geometry provides cast shadows without new occluder authoring, at the cost of imperfect correspondence with art. A session toggle avoids coupling the first lighting pass to scheduling.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision extends object, settings, input, and session rendering contracts; see `docs/asset-service.md` and `docs/input-system.md`.
- **Source:** Lighting implementation, user-confirmed short collision-outline shadows, and tracked authoring guidance in `docs/asset-service.md` and `README.md`.
- **Review timing:** Revisit when automatic time-of-day or lighting authoring expands.

## Enemy infighting retaliation

- **Decision:** Each enemy definition requires integer `infight_chance` from 0 through 100 inclusive; missing, null, fractional, and out-of-range values fail loading. The victim's value controls retaliation. All nine local enemy definitions use 30 in the ignored raw and generated trees, not as a fallback. Remove the former `enemyInfightingChance` game setting and migrate other checkouts' enemy data before regenerating assets.
- **Override:** At startup, `GameData.withAllInfightChances(int chance)` returns a copy with every enemy definition updated in one pass; `GameData.withEnemyInfightChance(String enemyId, int chance)` returns a copy with only the specified enemy updated. Both validate the inclusive 0–100 range, leave the original unchanged, and can be chained. The former public `ActorsData` helper is replaced by package-private copy implementation. No configuration or gameplay behavior changes; awareness retains its boolean mode opt-out and the separate demo disables infighting.
- **Behavior:** Positive nonlethal enemy-on-enemy melee or arrow damage gives one chance roll only when the attacker is a valid visible retaliation target within the existing three-times-detection-radius range. Facing does not limit retaliation; solid cover blocks sight. Zero disables retaliation and 100 always succeeds for eligible hits. Damage itself is unchanged, as are player and NPC behaviors. Dredge requires live sight for enemy retaliation; its special player-arrow pursuit remains unchanged.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Per-enemy percentages control incidental infighting without changing damage or existing player and NPC retaliation; startup copies can adjust all enemies or one enemy without changing assets.
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md); enemy data authoring in `docs/asset-service.md`.
- **Source:** User-directed enemy infighting threshold.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if retaliation eligibility or settings change.

## Map actor awareness

- **Decision:** NPCs and enemies own query-only circles centered on their physical feet; the player is detectable but has no awareness circle. Mandatory `data/characters.json` and `data/enemies.json`, loaded through `GameConsts.DATA_PATH`, are each top-level maps keyed by spawn key. Both files must be present and may be empty; spawn role selects the map. Every definition requires `detection_bounds` with a positive finite `radius` in pixels and a `behavior_graph` key. Enemy definitions also require a string `detection_bounds.shape` of `FULL_CIRCLE` or `FORWARD_SEMICIRCLE`; the NPC schema is unchanged. Graphs are reusable across definitions; sample keys are `wander` and `wander_attack`. The old `decision_graph` field is not accepted and has no alias; migrate the ignored raw actor files, preserve the values, and regenerate runtime assets. Missing or invalid data fails loading without a fallback. Local NPC and enemy radii are 96 and 160 pixels, not code defaults. Author the files in the local raw data tree and regenerate runtime assets.
- **Actor authoring:** NPCs retain a loadout; enemies require a nullable top-level `weapon` instead. Each requires a nonblank `name` and positive integer `max_health` for initial and maximum spawn health. Animation `atlas_key` selects the appropriate sprite prefix. Authored move speed drives dynamic bodies; sounds remain authored. Tiled `NpcSpawn` and `EnemySpawn` points prefer `spawnKey`, falling back to legacy `actorId` when no explicit key is authored; local keys are `basic_npc` and `brainless_undead` respectively.
- **Superseding source choice:** The role-specific character and enemy files replace the duplicated `actors.json` source; there is no fallback to it. Preserve graph keys and detection radii when moving entries, and retain map actor `max_health` while adding names. The player schema is unchanged. The ignored raw and runtime copies are migrated locally; other checkouts must migrate their own ignored data and regenerate runtime assets. This source change does not alter awareness behavior.
- **Behavior and tradeoff:** Range queries use physical feet centers, include the circle boundary, and return matching actor roles without self. Circles do not generate contacts. The NPC only wanders. A per-actor Wander/Engage/Investigate FSM uses simple explicit Java transitions and selects the behavior graph for its current mode. Enemies acquire a living player within their authored feet-centered detection shape with feet-to-feet sight. `FORWARD_SEMICIRCLE` uses an inclusive forward half-plane; `FULL_CIRCLE` detects in all directions. Migration assigns `FULL_CIRCLE` to `dredge` and `FORWARD_SEMICIRCLE` to the other existing enemies; spawn identity does not select the shape. Retaliation ignores facing. Only the Hoard Dredge guard normally returns to spawn on detection loss; a damaging player arrow instead provokes pursuit beyond normal sight and range until solid body contact or the player is no longer live, then normal detection and combat rules resume. Engage follows the detectable player at authored move speed, faces along the dominant axis, and records the player's last seen world-space feet coordinates. On loss of detection, Investigate steers directly to that point and returns to Wander at at most 8 logical pixels or after 2 seconds, whichever comes first. A detectable player can be reacquired on any tick. Only live sight can request melee: with a primary melee attack and distance at most 32 logical pixels, the actor stops, faces the player, and attacks when ready, waiting in place on cooldown. Perception updates during an attack, but motion and facing remain committed until it ends. Solid non-actor fixtures block sight, including at the ray origin, while sensors and actors do not. Vertical wins facing ties; coincident centers preserve facing. Existing combat handles animation, cooldown, and damage. This adds no pathfinding or saved data. Queries use the last completed physics/teleport snapshot.
- **Supersession:** The historical statements that the NPC only wanders and that behavior retains no target references are superseded for limited retaliation and retaliation-target retention by the current [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md) contract. Confirmed nonlethal player melee damage provokes ordinary enemies alongside existing arrow retaliation, regardless of facing but subject to three detection radii and solid cover. Cooldowns and committed windups remain in force; NPC melee behavior is unchanged; the Hoard guard's arrow-provoked pursuit is specified in the Hoard arena decision above. NPCs still have no passive aggression. The physics debug overlay draws each enemy's authored detection shape without visualizing occlusion; NPC circles are unchanged.
- **Combat timing prototype (`hoard-mode-combat-test` branch):** This supplement supersedes the immediate-ready attack timing above on this branch, not the nonattack awareness behavior. Each eligible default/base AI primary attack opportunity samples a decision delay once: 25–100 ms for melee and 150–300 ms for archers; time before eligibility does not count. Losing sight, leaving melee range, changing target or weapon, or losing readiness resets the opportunity. A ready melee actor between 24 and 32 logical pixels can step closer while deciding; at or within 24 pixels it plants, and on cooldown it waits. Once committed, it holds its opening pose as needed so the hit frame is no earlier than 280 ms into playback; slower authored windups remain slower. The sensor lasts through the authored follow-through and is removed at attack completion. Recovery then locks movement for at least another 160 ms; its duration is `max(160 ms, 400 ms − authored hit-to-end time)`, so follow-through counts toward the 400 ms window. Committed melee attacks continue on loss of sight or detection range.
- **Prototype archers and scope:** Archers also wait for a sampled opportunity, approach outside half their detection radius while deciding or recovering from projectile playback, and plant to draw. Halfway through the draw they snapshot a constant-velocity intercept including remaining draw time and flight, face that point, and stop tracking; if the intercept is unreachable within projectile lifetime they aim directly. Live sight must renew the request until release, so losing it cancels even a locked draw without starting cooldown. A new draw cannot truncate active projectile playback. There is no cap on concurrent eligible attacks. These AI changes apply wherever the branch's AI graphs run, not only inside Hoard mode; player attack behavior is unchanged. Timing is prototype tuning, not an asset or settings contract.
- **Graph naming:** The neutral `io.mindspice.lute.engine.behaviorgraph` package replaces `engine.ai.decisiongraph`, and `BehaviorGraphs` replaces `Graphs`. Java accessors are `behaviorGraph()` and `behaviorGraphKey()`. Behavior graph is the broader name because graphs select actions as well as decisions; this rename does not deliver generic non-actor traversal.
- **Justification and alternatives:** Query-only sensors support spatial awareness without affecting combat or movement contacts. Persistent modes allow a bounded search after sight is lost; direct steering avoids pathfinding. Explicit transitions keep mode ownership separate from graph choices without introducing an actor FSM library. Player-owned sensing and further perceived item types are not part of this behavior.
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md).
- **Source:** User-selected NPC/enemy circles, player detection, enemy line-of-sight facing, and Wander/Engage/Investigate behavior. This supersedes the prior no-actor-FSM decision.
- **Review timing:** Revisit when perception or actor behavior expands.

## Session world time

- **Decision:** Each `GameScreen` owns an enabled, queryable world clock starting at day 0, 06:00 (21,600 total in-game seconds). Optional numeric `dayLengthMinutes` in game settings specifies simulation minutes per in-game day; omission defaults to 24, giving 60 in-game seconds per simulation second. Fractional values are accepted; explicit null, non-numbers, non-finite or non-positive values, and values producing a non-finite or non-positive derived rate are rejected. `GameConsts` supplies the setting to the clock. It exposes total seconds, zero-based day index, and fractional minute of day, and its priority of -100 advances it before default-priority systems.
- **Lifecycle and tradeoff:** The clock follows the screen's simulation updates, including the 1/30-second render delta clamp; hidden or paused rendering does not advance it. Named travel and hide/show retain the clock, while a new screen starts a new clock. The clock freezes during Hoard visits and resumes outside; regular maps are unchanged. This is session time, not wall time or saved state.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision establishes the session clock contract; future uses are uncommitted [horizon ideas](horizon-ideas.md).
- **Source:** World-time system implementation and focused clock, screen lifecycle, and map travel tests.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if time rate, ownership, scheduling, or persistence changes.

## Tiled sensors, teleports, and spawn markers

- **Decision:** The sensor shape contract supports filled rectangles, circles/ellipses, and simple polygons, including rotation, for `Sensor` and `Teleport` objects on orthogonal maps. Objects belong to `Trigger` or `Triggers` object layers and have unique sensor names. Trigger layers and ancestor groups retain zero offsets and unit parallax. Rectangles and ellipses have positive dimensions; invalid, self-intersecting, or degenerate polygons are rejected. Non-circular ellipses use a fixed eight-sided approximation. Concave polygons and simple polygons with more than eight vertices use multiple convex fixtures but remain one logical detector, with no duplicate events and the same teleport reconciliation as a single-fixture detector. Points and polylines are not detector shapes; text and tile objects are excluded. `Sensor.sensorType` uses `GENERAL`, and teleports also emit `GENERAL` sensor enter and exit events. Typed events carry the sensor name, type, and player, and only the player's non-sensor fixtures participate. The map owner cleans up sensor objects.
- **Teleport and spawn contract:** `targetMap` of `THIS` is case-insensitive and teleports in place to a unique `TELEPORT`-category `SpawnPoint`, stopping velocity, preserving facing, and resetting interpolation. A named map ID, including the current map ID, instead stages and validates a replacement runtime and rebuilds it after the ECS and physics update; `targetSpawn` must name a `PLAYER` or `TELEPORT` point in that map. Failed preparation logs the error and retains the current runtime. Arrival portals require exit before reentry. A `SpawnPoint` is a zero-size point. A data-only `SpawnArea` is an unrotated positive-size rectangle; a behavior-enabled area supports an unrotated positive-size rectangle or valid polygon. Both use a Tiled object name unique among spawn markers and a `spawnType` of `PLAYER`, `TELEPORT`, `NPC`, `ENEMY`, `OBJECT`, or `ITEM`. At most one `PLAYER` point is allowed; it creates the initial player. NPC and ENEMY points create map actors, preferring `spawnKey` with legacy `actorId` fallback when no explicit key is authored. OBJECT and ITEM points require `spawnKey` and create objects; data-only areas do not spawn actors, while behavior-enabled enemy areas support spawning. PLAYER, NPC, ENEMY, OBJECT, and ITEM points require the Tiled `facing` enum (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT`, or `RIGHT`); the Tiled class supplies the `DOWN` default, not Java. The local sample NPC faces `LEFT`. A new player uses the PLAYER marker's facing, but named and in-place travel preserve the live player's facing. TELEPORT points do not use facing.
- **Shared spawn metadata:** The Tiled `SpawnPoint` and `SpawnArea` classes contain a nested `spawnInfo` property of class `SpawnInfo`, with `spawnType`, `spawnKey`, and `facing`. Legacy flat values override the corresponding nested values when present; actor `actorId` fallback remains available for an unset key. Java `MapTriggers.SpawnInfo` shares the object name and spawn type; point position, key and facing and area bounds remain on their respective records. The Tiled spawn type enum retains `TELEPORT`. Migrate ignored raw maps and project definitions by marker role, then regenerate runtime assets. The shared metadata avoids duplicating name and type in Java. The arena's required polygons and behavior key are documented in the hoard arena decision above.
- **Object authoring:** A required shared `objects.atlas` is generated from `raw_assets/sprites/objects/`. Required `raw_assets/data/objects.json` is keyed by `spawnKey`; every definition requires explicitly nullable `animation`, `collision`, and `emissive`. Null animation selects the static region named exactly by `spawnKey`; nonnull animation uses the player `AnimationData` schema and its `atlas_key` selects indexed frames, with `sound_frame` unused. Collision bounds use bottom-left sprite pixels. Center the sprite on the point and retain the fixture's local offset while applying facing to both: DOWN 0°, RIGHT 90°, UP 180°, LEFT -90°. Collision rotation adds to facing without independently rotating the art. The local `test_tiled` FireSpawn at Tiled x=134, y=164 uses `campfire1`: six row-major 32×32 frames from the old 128×64 sheet, excluding its last two blank cells, saved as `raw_assets/sprites/objects/campfire1/campfire1_00.png` through `_05.png`. Its animation uses `atlas_key` `campfire1/campfire1`, speed 1, frame interval 0.1, and sound frame 0; its bounds are `{x1: 4, y1: 2, x2: 28, y2: 14}`. Regenerate runtime assets after updating the ignored raw tree; `docs/asset-service.md` includes the JSON example.
- **Session and ownership:** The gameplay session retains live health, loadout, and facing across named travel and hide/show; the rebuilt player receives fresh combat, movement, and input state. A fresh session starts from player JSON defaults. Map assets load synchronously; rebuilding a named current map reuses registered assets rather than hot-reloading them from disk. This is not save persistence.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Named markers provide explicit destinations and typed sensor events. Staging protects the live map from destination preparation failures, while rebuilding outside the update avoids changing the world during physics. Collision ellipses use the same true-circle versus eight-sided ellipse rule and Tiled rotation convention, with collision-specific scaling and offsets. The fixed ellipse approximation and convex fixture composition support the approved shapes while retaining one logical detector. Requiring exit before reentry prevents destination-trigger loops; behavior-enabled enemy areas support spawning, and the hoard arena requires four polygons.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision establishes the sensor, teleport, and spawn authoring contract; `docs/asset-service.md` describes authoring.
- **Source:** User-approved sensor and teleport work, the approved PR #12 shape contract, and map travel implementation `6b048836`.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if sensor, spawn, or map-transition contracts change.

## Input settings

- **Decision:** Startup loads `assets/input.setting.json` after game settings and before creating the shared input manager. The file must contain `keyboard` and `controller` object maps and may contain a `mouse` object map; each may be empty. Keyboard identifiers are exact, case-sensitive LibGDX `Input.Keys` key constant names, such as `SPACE`, `SHIFT_LEFT`, `SHIFT_RIGHT`, and `NUM_1`; values in all maps are exact `InputAction` names. Unknown names, non-key constants such as `META` masks, duplicate aliases for one physical key, and other invalid, duplicate, or missing data fail startup rather than falling back to hard-coded bindings.
- **Default weapon-cycle binding:** Tracked `assets/input.setting.json` maps `R` to `CYCLE_WEAPON`. The action uses the optional ordered player loadout cycle rather than hard-coding sword and bow; its temporary testing scope and guards are specified in [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md). This provides a lightweight test switch without introducing inventory. Revisit when weapon management is defined.
- **Controller scope:** Valid case-sensitive identifiers are `DPAD_UP`, `DPAD_DOWN`, `DPAD_LEFT`, `DPAD_RIGHT`, `FACE_SOUTH`, `FACE_WEST`, `FACE_NORTH`, `FACE_EAST`, `BACK`, `START`, `L1`, `R1`, `L2`, `R2`, `LEFT_STICK_BUTTON`, `RIGHT_STICK_BUTTON`, `LEFT_STICK_UP`, `LEFT_STICK_DOWN`, `LEFT_STICK_LEFT`, and `LEFT_STICK_RIGHT`. Defaults bind the D-pad and left stick to movement, south/west face buttons to primary/secondary attack, east to sprint, and north to the arena weapon cycle. An empty map avoids controller backend access for gameplay polling. The file does not add actions or support hot reload; the menu can apply saved local keyboard and controller overrides.
- **Mouse scope:** The optional mouse map accepts exactly `MButton1` through `MButton5`, case-sensitively, for left, right, middle, back, and forward respectively. These bind mouse buttons to actions, not keyboard aliases. The shipped map is empty. `InputManager` and `MouseManager` capture button pairs for both the shared action queue and existing positional mouse caches, including balancing releases; scroll behavior is unchanged.
- **Runtime contract:** LWJGL3 standard-mapped pads share the existing gameplay queue with simultaneous keyboard input. The first connected pad supporting a configured control retains ownership until disconnect, when its delivered actions are released and another supported pad can be selected. `GameScreen` polls before ECS only while visible and unpaused. The first sample after selection or lifecycle reset ignores controls held until release. Stick directions are digital, with 0.50 press and at-or-below 0.35 release thresholds; diagonals use the existing normalized movement speed. Each physical binding delivers its own action pairs without aggregation or ECS changes. A complete tap between polls is missed.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Authored bindings replace hard-coded startup keys and use logical controls instead of device-specific codes. Keyboard names use ALL_CAPS enum-like constant names to match controller identifiers rather than LibGDX display labels. Polling supports mapped pads through the existing queue but cannot capture between-sample taps.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision establishes the input settings contract; `docs/input-system.md` describes authoring and startup.
- **Source:** User-directed input settings and gamepad work and PR #24 keyboard naming correction.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if controller support or input delivery changes.

## Cache-only input listeners

- **Decision:** `KeyListener` and `MouseListener` are concrete, independently owned input caches. Managers deliver to these caches, not immediate input listener callbacks. Controllers retain their own key queues. `ControllerSystem` updates movement direction directly without emitting `Event.Player.Moved`. Mouse position updates the latest cached position; selected buttons and scroll enter its queue.
- **Justification and alternative:** Deferred queue consumption preserves the existing cache-based gameplay path without a separate immediate-delivery listener API. The former callback listener implementations and cache class names are removed rather than retained as compatibility aliases; this intentionally breaks the old API.
- **Caveats:** LibGDX event callbacks and `KeyActionConsumer`/`MouseEventConsumer` queue-drain consumers remain. Consumers run when callers consume or poll, not directly from managers. Queues are bounded, so overflow can lose transitions. `releaseHeldInputs()` queues balancing releases for enabled listeners selecting each captured action; callers must drain them outside event dispatch on the main thread.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision is the current input-listener contract; `docs/input-system.md` describes its application and lifecycle.
- **Source:** User-directed input listener simplification and user-approved issue #6 movement-event correction.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if the input delivery contract changes.

## Common animation authoring schema

- **Decision:** Shared `AnimationData` uses `speed`, `atlas_key`, `sound_frame`, and `frame_interval` in player and weapon JSON. Tiled's nested `Animation` class uses camelCase `speed`, `atlasKey`, `soundFrame`, and `frameInterval`; `AnimatedPhysicObject.animation` contains the class. Players start in `IDLE` and animated environmental objects in `DEFAULT`; neither type is configured in Tiled. Player animation values come from JSON. Each map manifest supplies one `atlasAsset` for environmental objects. The composite-character contract replaces its former use for player visuals; phases 1–4 are implemented and accepted by [final integration validation](../reviews/2026-11-16-composite-character-integration.md). Explicit placement animation fields override the corresponding tile fields; `FixedTmxMapLoader` preserves the tile values for omitted fields.
- **Authoring:** Keep animation fields, types, and meaning synchronized between Tiled and JSON despite the naming convention difference; values are independently authored. Do not use `keyFrames`. Player and tree `atlas_key` values identify atlas regions. The former weapon `NONE`-only rendering limitation is superseded by the composite-character contract. Only weapon attacks consume `sound_frame`. Player move and damage sounds use `sword_hit.wav` as data-only placeholders, without loading or playback. The tree uses a 0.1-second frame interval and speed 0.35.
- **Source:** User-directed animation schema and asset authoring alignment.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if animation data ownership or authoring changes.

## Animation-driven attack lifetime

- **Decision:** Weapon definitions use `attack_stats` with distinct primary and secondary attack records, each containing animation, bounds, stats, collision type, and its own attack and hit sound paths and logical IDs. Each attack owns `sounds.attack` and `sounds.hit` paths; existing WAV files are approved for reuse. For melee attacks, `stats.hit_frame` starts the attack sensor; animation completion normally ends the attack, though fresh input for a ready attack can replace current playback even while the old attack is cooling down. This supersedes the former single-attack schema. Bow projectile execution is specified in [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md). Frames are zero-based: frame 0 is immediate at animation start, and frame 2 starts the third frame. `animation.speed` scales the animation clock and therefore both frame timing and total attack lifetime. The independent `attack_duration` timer and unused `hitDelay` are removed.
- **Ownership:** Player JSON supplies the base animation `frame_interval` in seconds; the equipped weapon's animation supplies its attack `frame_interval`. Both must be finite and positive. Combat passes the attack interval, animation speed, sound frame, and resolved attack-sound ID to `Animation2D` when accepting an attack. Animation emits the sound once when playback reaches or crosses `animation.sound_frame`; it does not inspect weapon definitions or manage fixtures. `EntityFactory` validates hit and sound frame limits against every facing direction when composing the entity. Animation revalidates those limits if a previously seen atlas identity is replaced. Combat reads animation progress and owns fixture creation, damage, and cleanup. An accepted attack can be active before its sensor exists. Hit sounds are loaded but not emitted.
- **Ordering and tradeoff:** The animation FSM and playback update before combat, and combat updates before physics. Accepted input configures the attack in the combat pass; its animation begins on the following update. Frame 0 is relative to that animation start. Physics retains ordinary fixed ticks with no forced contact refresh: an entire active interval skipped by an update, or shorter than a physics tick, can produce no hit.
- **Affected specifications and sources:** `assets/data/weapons.json`, `WeaponsData`, `Combat`, `CombatWeapon`, `Animation2D`, `AnimationState`, `AnimationSystem`, `CombatSystem`, `GameScreen`, and `EntityFactory`.
- **Source:** User-directed weapon asset and animation-timing refactor. This supersedes the former explicit combat-duration decision.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if attack timing, animation ownership, or physics contact guarantees change.

## Fighter attack cooldown

- **Decision:** Require finite, positive `stats.cooldown_seconds` for each primary and secondary attack. Runtime cooldowns are independent per fighter, stable weapon definition ID, and slot, with no global timer. They age before input independently of playback, including while another weapon is equipped. Switching away and back does not reset the original cooldown; fresh map combat state clears them. Melee acceptance and arrow release start the corresponding cooldown; bow draw and cancellation do not. Natural completion does not clear it.
- **Ordering and tradeoff:** Fresh input for a ready attack can replace current playback even while the old attack is still cooling down, without buffering input. Resolve queued physics hits first, remove any old melee sensor, and leave existing arrows in flight. Normal movement is unchanged. Bow draw checks primary readiness and aim reserves input while held; cooldowns add no swapping restriction beyond existing attack-state guards. Replacement can truncate not-yet-reached hit and sound cues even when the old cooldown is long.
- **Asset migration:** Add the field to every attack in externally provisioned `raw_assets/data/weapons.json` and regenerate runtime assets. The locally tuned seconds are sword primary `0.30`, secondary `0.24`; bow primary `0.25`, secondary `0.32`; and unarmed `0.32` for both. These asset edits are local and untracked.
- **Justification and alternatives:** Separating attack cadence from animation completion permits responsive follow-up attacks without changing authored cues or adding an input buffer. A different ready attack can truncate playback and cues regardless of the old attack's cooldown duration.
- **Affected specification:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md).
- **Source:** User-directed attack cooldown contract.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if attack cadence, cue timing, or recovery replacement changes.

## Arrow flight near north

- **Decision:** Use one world-Y parabola through the aim point, tangent-facing and traveled at arc-length speed. Taper base lift smoothly from zero at 10° to full at 25° from the actual north aim vector. Within that transition band, expire at the apex if the arrow has not cleared the launch strip; otherwise continue on the same parabola until impact or lifetime expiry. Centered aim follows the owner's facing in a straight line.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** The taper avoids a northward arc reversing through the shooter while preserving lift away from north. Conditional apex expiry occurs after the aim point; impact or lifetime expiry can still end flight before it. In the transition band, apex progress is `0.5 + travel.y / (8 * lift) > 1` because lift is at most `0.15 * distance` and `travel.y` is at least `cos(25°) * distance`. Other unaccepted approaches and their tradeoffs are recorded in [Horizon Ideas](horizon-ideas.md).
- **Affected specification:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md).
- **Source:** Current `ArrowFlight` implementation and user live acceptance of the UP behavior.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if projectile flight or collision semantics change.

## Projectile collision geometry

- **Decision:** Player, NPC, enemy, object, and projectile JSON require `collision` with `shape`, `bounds`, and `rotation`; only object collision may be explicitly null. Shapes are `ELLIPSE`, `RECT`, `CIRCLE`, and `TRIANGLE`. Bounds use local pixel coordinates `x1`, `y1`, `x2`, `y2`, with positive dimensions and equal width and height for circles. Rotation is finite counterclockwise degrees around the bounds center, combined with object facing or projectile flight tangent, without independently rotating art. Actors and objects use native circle fixtures; projectile circles use 32-vertex sweep polygons. Ellipses use eight vertices; triangles have a bottom base and top-midpoint apex. Projectile bounds' unrotated top midpoint anchors at the art tip, with X as full width and Y as length behind the tip. Sweeps use local fixture queries; the yellow debug outline depicts instantaneous geometry and does not perform another collision check.
- **Migration and scope:** Replace `collision_bounds` in the ignored raw player, characters, enemies, objects, and projectiles JSON files with the required schema, then regenerate runtime assets. All migrated entries use `ELLIPSE` and rotation 0; the formerly triangular iron arrow uses bounds `(0, 0, 10, 8.5)`. Hitboxes, Tiled collision, arrow trajectory, and art are unchanged. See `docs/asset-service.md` for provisioning.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** The sweep preserves projectile width and prevents tunneling. It requires more work than a ray or per-frame overlap test, but neither alternative provides both properties.
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md).
- **Source:** User-directed projectile collision decision.
- **Review timing:** Revisit only if profiling many projectiles indicates a cost.

## Settings and weapon asset ownership

- **Decision:** `assets/game.settings.json` supplies the existing `GameConsts` values and map, data, sound, music, object, and atlas directory prefixes. It requires `startupMap` as a map name, not a path, and `projectPath` as the root Tiled `.tiled-project` filename. The provisioned local example uses `hoard_mode` and `game.tiled-project`; the asset generator's configurable project filename must align with `projectPath`. `GameCore.create()` loads and validates settings before constructing runtime services or loading game data. Invalid settings fail startup without partially applying values. Static field defaults retain standalone-tool compatibility; runtime startup requires the settings file.
- **Ownership:** Map manifests declare map-owned assets. The former map-atlas-only player and unsupported separate-weapon-visual decisions are superseded: characters use separate shared character, equipment, and weapons atlases as specified in [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md). Weapon regions retain melee/ranged prefixes in the common weapons atlas. Shared asset loading, loadout support, and composite rendering are implemented and accepted by [final integration validation](../reviews/2026-11-16-composite-character-integration.md).
- **Loading:** Initial loading queues attack and hit sounds for both attack slots of every configured weapon, including unarmed, using `weapon/<id>/<primary|secondary>/<attack|hit>` IDs. Each slot independently authors its paths, even when the existing WAV files are shared. Hit sounds are loaded but not played. Paths remain relative to the configured sound directory; weapon definitions do not require map sound entries. The composite-character contract adds runtime equipment switching, but not inventory, UI, or automatic asset lifetime management.
- **Validation boundary:** Validate JSON, settings, and asset definitions on entry, and validate geometry and cue limits where configuration meets loaded assets. Trust validated values in runtime records and update loops rather than repeating checks. Optional state, gameplay conditions, and asset lifecycle, type, and loading failures remain meaningful checks. This keeps the closed runtime path simple without weakening external boundaries.

## Loadout and combat ownership

- **Decision:** `Loadout` owns equipped equipment and weapon selection, with its immutable per-entity catalogue and equipment validator. `Combat` owns pending and active attack state and the active attack snapshot, sensor, and hits; neither selection nor a `Combat` reference belongs in the other component. `LoadoutSystem` coordinates guarded switches, rejecting pending or active combat before selection validation or mutation. Combat is optional for loadout switching and rendering; the compositor reads the loadout directly.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Separating equipped items from attack state lets passive and rendering-only entities use loadouts without dummy combat state. Component setters still validate selections but do not enforce the combat guard, so guarded gameplay callers must use the system. The existing catalogue and validator remain in `Loadout` rather than changing the asset-validation architecture.
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md); usage appears in `docs/asset-service.md`.
- **Source:** User-directed PR #15 ownership correction.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if equipment or attack ownership changes.

## Composite character rendering

- **Decision:** Compose the base character, equipped head, body, hands, feet, and weapon into an offscreen cell per visible character. Back to front, UP uses weapon, base, feet, body, hands, head; DOWN, LEFT, and RIGHT use base, feet, body, hands, head, weapon. An unarmed character omits the weapon. Batch source layers by their actual atlas texture page while preserving that order, then render each completed character as one world-depth-sorted sprite alongside ordinary objects. Preserve transparent edges with correct premultiplied-alpha handling.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Drawing head after hands corrects the reported glove-over-helmet clipping without changing the weapon's existing position. This fixed direction-based order is not a per-frame sorting system or a guarantee for all future gear. Page batching reduces texture changes while isolated cells preserve each character's layer ordering and world overlap. A global painter pass over independent character layers can break overlap; independently sorting weapons also breaks the character as a world unit. Offscreen targets and alpha handling add resource and rendering complexity.
- **Caveats:** Shared animation timing aligns layers. Map NPCs use composition and enemies use baked body art with weapon variants; NPC and enemy points spawn map-owned actors. Phases 1–4 are implemented and accepted by [final integration validation](../reviews/2026-11-16-composite-character-integration.md).
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md).
- **Source:** User-selected offscreen composition and loadout contract.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if rendering or character composition requirements change.

## Character canvas and logical bounds

- **Decision:** Render the complete 128×128 source canvas at pixel scale, including padding and overhang, around an explicitly configured centered 64×64 logical character area. Its canvas offset relative to the logical transform is -32 pixels per axis; spawning and weapon anchors remain logical. Do not infer logical size from canvas size, crop padding, or downsample to 64×64.
- **Bounds:** Player `collision` describes the 24×10 feet ellipse and `hitbox_bounds` the separate 32×50 damage ellipse, both in bottom-left logical-local pixels and scaled to world units once. Non-circular ellipses use the existing eight-vertex Box2D approximation. The persistent hurtbox is a sensor, excluded from movement blocking and map triggers. Attack sensors use it instead of solids for explicit hurtbox targets; generic full-sprite targets keep their fallback.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Source canvas size includes transparent padding and weapon or animation overhang; treating it as logical size doubles or displaces the character, while shrinking it loses the intended pixel scale. Separate feet and damage shapes fit their different roles without enlarging movement or trigger contact.
- **Caveats:** The user approved the initial fitted bounds as the implementation contract, but live review and any resulting tuning remain pending. Focused automated validation passed; see the [canvas and hitboxes validation report](../reviews/2026-11-16-character-canvas-hitboxes-validation.md) for final gate evidence. Historical integration results do not validate this correction.
- **Affected specifications:** [Composite Characters](composite-characters.md).
- **Source:** Explicit user correction and approval of initial fitted bounds.
- **Review timing:** Review the fitted bounds in live play before updating the PR; revisit the contract if character authoring changes.

## Custom asset service and logical keys

- **Decision:** Replace the AssetManager-backed service with a directly owning service whose logical identity is resource class and logical ID. Store entries in four typed maps selected directly by class, with a separate ordered loading `queue`. The maps alone track registered assets; diagnostics and disposal iterate them without a redundant ownership index or a cross-type insertion-order guarantee. Asset records define loads; runtime audio events and animation components carry IDs. Music and sound manifest keys remain IDs; the manifest name is the map and its single atlas ID. The service supports sounds, music, atlases, and tiled maps.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Explicit keys separate runtime identity from file paths and make ownership and unloading direct. The standalone comparison favored typed lookup in the measured workloads and showed a separate benefit from avoiding scans of loaded entries during queue updates; it does not establish a universal speedup or gameplay improvement. The complete composite implementation remains test-only for comparison. Unlike AssetManager, the service has no background loading or reference counting: queue updates synchronously load at most one entry, while an explicit load handles only its requested entry. Repeating the same key and path is idempotent; conflicting paths fail while the key exists.
- **Caveats:** Load on the render thread. Failed loads remove their entries for explicit retry; a `LoadingScreen` update failure is propagated and latched until `show()` resets the screen and queues its definitions again. Unloading cancels queued work or disposes the loaded resource; users must stop using it and its dependents first. Different keys for the same path own independent loads. Direct atlas and map loaders retain their textures until ownership transfers to the completed resource and dispose already-created textures on runtime failure; `DirectTexture` disposes its GL handle if loading fails during construction. Service disposal is idempotent.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision establishes the asset identity, loading, and ownership contract; `docs/asset-service.md` describes usage and migration.
- **Source:** User-directed replacement of AssetManager-backed asset handling; `.internal-dev/reviews/asset-storage-benchmark-comparison.md`.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if asset identity, loading, or ownership contracts change, or relevant measurement evidence changes.

## Generated terrain tilesheet extrusion

- **Decision:** Keep terrain authoring in `raw_assets/` unchanged. `generate-runtime-assets.sh` extrudes a one-pixel repeated RGBA border per tile into generated PNGs for grid-based, single-image terrain tilesets in external TSX and inline TMX definitions. Generated metadata uses margin 1 and spacing 2 and reflects the generated image reference and dimensions and actual grid columns and tilecount. Missing source margin or spacing means zero; valid nondefault source values are read as authored. Actual source image dimensions determine the grid; invalid geometry and conflicting explicit columns fail generation, while stale tilecount is normalized. Never write raw assets.
- **Map atlas and ownership:** Pack used grid terrain from multiple sheets, including placed tile objects and animation frames, into the map's existing manifest atlas with its object regions. Record each map's selected local IDs on its TMX tileset references, not in shared TSX definitions. Preserve tile IDs, properties, animation, and collision. Image collections use the object atlas; image-layer images remain separate. Terrain pages have a 2048×2048 maximum and can overflow, so one page is not guaranteed. Empty and terrain-only maps still have an atlas. Chunked tile layers are unsupported.
- **Loading and fallback:** The runtime loader borrows the atlas and resolves marked terrain by reserved region name `__lute_tile/<firstgid>` and tileset-local tile ID index. Qualifying keys by tileset preserves distinct animation frames and placements when GIDs overlap; regenerate runtime assets produced by the earlier GID-only atlas format. Missing or invalid regions fail rather than falling back. Without an atlas, standalone loading uses generated padded sheets. The atlas owner must outlive maps and consumers borrowing its textures. All generated outputs follow existing ownership and transaction rules.
- **Justification and tradeoff:** Packing used terrain together can reduce texture switches while retaining the existing atlas ownership boundary and padded sheets for standalone loads. Overflow may still require texture changes. Generated padding reduces bleed without changing raw art, but does not resolve every cause of tile seams.
- **Affected specifications:** This decision establishes the terrain extrusion contract; `README.md` and `docs/asset-service.md` describe authoring and generation.
- **Source:** User-directed automated terrain tilesheet extrusion contract.
- **Review timing:** Revisit if terrain authoring, generation, or runtime sampling requirements change.
