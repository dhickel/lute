# Composite Characters

## Purpose
A character consists of a base sprite and equipped head, body, hands, feet, and weapon layers. Phases 1–4 are implemented and accepted by [final integration validation](../reviews/2026-11-16-composite-character-integration.md).

## Intended Contract
### Composition and loadout
The player default loadout uses the basic item in all four equipment slots and `basic_sword`. The player loadout object is required, but each of its head, body, hands, feet, and weapon IDs may be omitted or null; any non-null ID must not be blank. All four armor slots are independently optional for the player and basic NPC, including a fully bare combination. The basic NPC defaults to basic body, hands, and feet, with no head item or weapon, but can equip compatible head armor. Every equipped armor layer must still have valid frames matching the actor's base animation. The demo enemy uses baked body and equipment art, starts with `basic_sword`, and has no separate armor. Its weapon can be changed through `LoadoutSystem`. `Loadout` owns equipped equipment and the selected weapon, retaining an immutable per-entity weapon catalogue and equipment-validation callback; it does not hold `Combat`. Its constructor takes equipment, weapon ID, catalogue, and validator. `getWeapon()` returns the selected definition and `getWeaponId()` returns null for unarmed. `setEquipment` and `setWeapon` validate selections but do not guard attack state.

For guarded gameplay switches, obtain `LoadoutSystem` from the engine and call `equip(entity, equipment)` or `equipWeapon(entity, weaponId)`. It rejects a pending or active attack before validation or mutation, returning false and leaving state unchanged. Otherwise it applies the component setter. Null selects the catalogue's unarmed weapon; an unknown idle ID throws without changing the selection. `Combat` is optional for loadout changes and rendering. `Combat` owns pending and active attack state, the active attack snapshot, sensor, and hits. `CombatSystem` requires `Loadout`, selects attacks from its weapon, and cleans up active attack state and any sensor on `Loadout` removal, both before and after sensor activation. Inventory and UI are not part of this work.

For temporary weapon testing, the external player JSON may include an ordered `loadout.weapon_cycle`, for example `"weapon_cycle": ["basic_sword", "basic_bow"]`. Configure this locally in `raw_assets/data/player.json` and regenerate runtime assets. Omitted, null, or empty cycles disable cycling; entries must be distinct, nonblank, and known weapon IDs. The default `R` binding triggers `CYCLE_WEAPON` on press only. It advances to the next entry, wrapping around, or selects the first entry when the current weapon is outside the list. Cycling is blocked while aiming, while an attack is pending or active, or when dead. Switching does not reset cooldowns. This is a lightweight test control, not inventory or weapon management.

Use one shared animation clock to align the facing, action, and frame of every visible layer. The compositor reads equipment and weapon from `Loadout`, without requiring `Combat`. A character without a weapon renders without the weapon layer.

### Assets
Build sprite assets from `raw_assets/sprites`: base frames in `character/player/` and `character/basic_npc/` share `character.atlas` with actor-prefixed regions. Baked enemy body and equipment sprites under `enemies/`, currently `brainless_undead/`, use a separate `enemies.atlas` with actor-prefixed regions. Equipment frames are organized as `equipment/<set>/<slot>/`, where slots are `head`, `body`, `hands`, and `feet`. The basic set requires all four nonempty slots. Gather sets by slot to generate separate head, body, hands, and feet atlases, preserving set prefixes in region names such as `basic/shot_up`. An optional `equipment/pack.json` supplies shared settings for these four atlases; reject nested packing overrides and unknown slots, except that `accessories` export folders are reported and skipped for now. Preserve their source files; do not pack an accessories atlas or add an equipable accessories layer. Weapons remain under `weapons/melee/basic_sword/` and `weapons/ranged/basic_bow/`. Generate separate character and common weapons atlases alongside the equipment atlases under `assets/atlases`. Sprite packing defaults to maximum page dimensions of 2048×2048. Optional `pack.json` settings inherit defaults for omitted dimensions and retain explicit overrides. Sprite pages must not mix source asset folders; reject `combineSubdirectories=true` overrides. A single folder may overflow onto multiple pages, without a guarantee that an individual animation remains on one page. Weapon region names retain their `melee` or `ranged` prefix in the common weapons atlas. Page counts and animation placement depend on the locally provisioned inputs and packing configuration; verify them when validating generated assets. The default arrow uses atlas key `projectiles/IronArrow`, sourced from `raw_assets/sprites/weapons/projectiles/IronArrow.png`. Its sprite must remain unrotated and untrimmed, with the configured tip inside its canvas. The generator can run without this optional source, but drawing an arrow then throws a missing-region exception; there is no substitute art.

Generated runtime resources are build outputs; raw assets are the source. Both trees are local and ignored by Git. A checkout requires the complete externally provisioned raw tree and `./generate-runtime-assets.sh` before asset-dependent execution. Map-owned environmental atlases remain separate and require combined subdirectory packing. The corrected raw base and each basic equipment set have 192 frames: death, idle, jab, shot, slash, and walk, each in four directions with eight frames. Sword and bow each have 160 frames; sword has no shot, bow has no slash, and neither has cast.

### Canvas and logical bounds
Each composite character frame canvas is 128×128 pixels. The character's authored logical size is 64×64 pixels, centered at canvas coordinates [32, 96] on each axis. Render the full canvas at pixel scale, including transparent padding and overhang; do not crop it or shrink it to the logical size. Relative to the 64×64 transform, the canvas render offset is -32 pixels on each axis. Spawn placement and weapon anchors use the logical area. `logical_size` is explicitly configured, not inferred from the source canvas.

Player JSON defines `logical_size` as `{ "width": 64, "height": 64 }`, `collision` as `{ "shape": "ELLIPSE", "bounds": { "x1": 20, "y1": 4, "x2": 44, "y2": 14 }, "rotation": 0 }`, and `hitbox_bounds` as `{ "x1": 16, "y1": 6, "x2": 48, "y2": 56 }`. Bounds are bottom-left-origin logical-local pixels and are converted to world units once. These define the 24×10 physical feet fixture and a separate 32×50 elliptical damage-receiving hurtbox. Non-circular Box2D ellipses use the existing eight-vertex approximation. The persistent hurtbox is a sensor, not a movement blocker or map-trigger contributor. Attack sensors target it rather than solid fixtures for explicit hurtbox targets; generic full-sprite targets retain their existing fallback.

### Weapon hitbox editor
A standalone JavaFX editor is registered as an included build in `settings.gradle` and launched from the repository root with `./gradlew :weapon-tester:run`. It reads the ignored raw player and weapon data and raw character, equipment, and weapon sprites. It previews UP, DOWN, LEFT, and RIGHT, distinguishing the player damage-receiving hurtbox, physical collider, and weapon attack hitbox overlays. The editor supports authoring the attack shape, extents, and directional offsets; for a weapon whose primary attack is a projectile, only the secondary attack is editable. An explicit save writes the changes to `raw_assets/data/weapons.json`. Runtime asset generation is separate: run `./generate-runtime-assets.sh` to propagate raw changes to `assets/`.

### Collision authoring
Player, NPC, enemy, object, and projectile definitions require `collision: { shape, bounds, rotation }`; only objects allow explicit null. Shapes are `ELLIPSE`, `RECT`, `CIRCLE`, and `TRIANGLE`; bounds are local pixels `x1`, `y1`, `x2`, `y2` with positive dimensions, and circles require equal width and height. Rotation is finite counterclockwise degrees about the bounds center, combined with object facing or projectile flight tangent without independently rotating art. Actor and object circles use native fixtures; projectile circles use 32-vertex sweep polygons. Ellipses use eight vertices, and triangles have a bottom base and top-midpoint apex. Hitboxes and Tiled collision are unchanged.

Migrate `raw_assets/data/player.json`, `characters.json`, `enemies.json`, `objects.json`, and `projectiles.json` from `collision_bounds` to this required schema. Existing migrated entries use `ELLIPSE` and rotation 0, including the formerly triangular iron arrow. Regenerate the ignored runtime copies with `./generate-runtime-assets.sh`; see `docs/asset-service.md` for provisioning.

### Attacks
Space is the default primary action: a sword slash triggers on press, while holding it with a bow starts aiming and releasing the last held primary binding fires once. Keyboard, gamepad, and mouse primary actions participate; mouse position does not move the target. Shift triggers the secondary jab on press, except while aiming. Without a weapon, both keys trigger a hand jab.

Weapon `attack_stats` contains separate `primary` and `secondary` records, each with animation, bounds, stats, collision type, and its own `sounds.attack` and `sounds.hit` paths. Sound IDs are `weapon/<id>/<primary|secondary>/<attack|hit>`. Initial loading includes both sounds for every configured attack, including unarmed. Existing WAV files are approved for reuse; hit sounds are loaded but not played. A bow shot plays its animation and launches the default arrow with damage scaled from the primary attack by the draw frame sampled at release, before recovery begins: frame 00 deals 25%, 01 deals 50%, 02 deals 75%, and 03 or later deals 100%. The arrow follows one unbroken world-Y parabola through the selected aim point if reachable, continuing on the same parabola beyond it until impact, lifetime expiry, or the conditional near-north apex expiry described below. A centered aim uses a straight path in the owner's facing direction. Its authored swept collision shape collides with terrain and hurtboxes, ignores its owner, and damages a living hit target. In-flight arrows are discarded when their owner or combat system is removed. Collision uses local fixture queries; the yellow debug outline shows instantaneous geometry and does not perform an additional collision check.

Health and authored and applied damage are whole numbers. Scalar damage rolls uniformly within ±10% after bow draw scaling and rounds once. A `{ "min": ..., "max": ... }` damage range instead rolls an inclusive whole number uniformly, applies bow draw scaling, and rounds once. Show the whole effective health loss, capped by the target's current health before the hit, on every damaged actor role as floating text. Color it green through yellow to red according to the target's post-hit current-to-maximum health ratio. The text moves upward and outward along the attack's committed facing, then fades quickly. A stylized bar spans the top of the gameplay viewport, with open sides and visually distinguished text. It displays the player's live current and maximum health and 24-hour game time, horizontally centered together as a group. Color the HP text using the same green-yellow-red health ratio as floating damage; do not add a colorful border.

The locally authored defaults give the player, `basic_npc`, and `brainless_undead` 50 maximum health outside the arena. Sword primary damage is 15–20 and secondary is 10–10; bow primary and secondary are 20–30. Unarmed attacks retain scalar damage 3. Partial bow draws scale the rolled primary damage by 25%, 50%, or 75%; full draw uses 100%. See `docs/asset-service.md` for provisioning the ignored assets.

Every primary and secondary attack requires a finite, positive `stats.cooldown_seconds`. Runtime cooldowns are independent per fighter, stable weapon definition ID, and attack slot; there is no global timer. They age during gameplay before input independently of playback, including while another weapon is equipped. Switching away and back does not reset the original cooldown; fresh map combat state clears cooldowns. Melee acceptance or arrow release starts the corresponding cooldown, not bow draw or cancellation. Bow draw checks primary-slot readiness; aim reserves input while held. Cooldowns add no restriction to weapon swapping beyond the existing attack-state guards. Natural animation completion does not clear cooldown. Without movement, fresh input for a ready attack can replace current playback even while the old attack is still cooling down, potentially truncating not-yet-reached hit or sound cues. During a committed player attack, directional input moves the player at full normal speed while the attack retains its original facing and hitbox orientation. Directional input cancels the remaining recovery once the attack's cooldown expires or its animation finishes; a melee hit sensor must also have participated in a physics step before cancellation. Queued physics hits resolve before sensor removal, and existing arrows continue. A ready follow-up requested during this handoff is retained; the first retained request wins. Movement continues through every fixed physics step, including batched steps and follow-up attacks, without a one-step limit. Releasing a deferred bow primary before draw starts cancels that request, and input invalidation or combat teardown clears pending requests. While aiming, movement continues to steer the crosshair instead of moving the player; same-frame bow decisions use the final held movement direction regardless of input edge order.

The externally provisioned `raw_assets/data/weapons.json` must be migrated to include this field for every attack, then regenerated into `assets/data/weapons.json` with `./generate-runtime-assets.sh`. Locally tuned seconds are sword primary `0.30` and secondary `0.24`, bow primary `0.25` and secondary `0.32`, and unarmed `0.32` in both slots. The raw and generated asset edits are local and untracked.

Projectile definitions are authored in `raw_assets/data/projectiles.json` and generated into `assets/data/projectiles.json`, under an ID-keyed `projectiles` map. At startup, `DataLoader` loads them through `GameConsts.DATA_PATH` alongside player and weapon data; `GameData` supplies the resolved data to gameplay. There is no classpath or bundled-resource fallback. The default ID is `iron_arrow`. Its required `collision` uses `shape`, `bounds`, and `rotation`. The unrotated bounds' top midpoint anchors at the art tip; X is full width and Y is length behind the tip, in pixels. The migrated `iron_arrow` uses `ELLIPSE`, bounds `{ "x1": 0, "y1": 0, "x2": 10, "y2": 8.5 }`, and rotation 0, replacing its former triangle. `speed` is world distance traveled along the flight curve per second, not projected speed along the aim line. `lifetime` is a cap in seconds; impact or conditional apex expiry can end flight sooner. Collision sweeps follow the subdivided flight curve with the last sweep clipped to the remaining lifetime. The arrow faces the curve's tangent. Base lift is `min(arc_height, 0.15 * aim distance)`, smoothly tapered according to the actual aim vector's angle from north: shots at or within 10° are straight, shots between 10° and 25° transition to full lift, and shots at or beyond 25° use full lift. Within the transition band, a shot expires at its apex if its horizontal displacement there has not cleared the launch strip, defined by 32 pixels plus the collision length and half-width. This apex is beyond the aim point: its progress is `0.5 + travel.y / (8 * lift) > 1`, since lift is at most `0.15 * distance` and `travel.y` is at least `cos(25°) * distance`. Impact or lifetime expiry can still end flight before the aim point. Geometry does not change with playback speed. Preserve the authored tip, arrow trajectory, and art.

Each attack's `bounds` requires `offset_up`, `offset_down`, `offset_left`, and `offset_right`, each an object with finite, required `x` and `y` coordinates in logical-local pixels. Current authored defaults are explicitly zero in both axes; omitted or null offsets are invalid. Rotate the right-facing geometry around the logical center first, then translate by the selected facing's offset. Positive X shifts right and positive Y shifts up regardless of facing; offsets do not change the rotation pivot or shape.

Aim starts one map tile from the player's center in the facing direction. Movement actions steer the crosshair instead of moving the player while aiming. The bow draws through shot frames 00–04 and holds 04. Releasing fires immediately once and starts frames 05–07, even on a quick release. The release pose faces the cardinal direction of the dominant target axis; vertical wins ties and a centered target preserves facing. If the aim point is reachable before impact or lifetime expiry, the arrow crosses it exactly and continues along the same parabola beyond it, potentially ending at the later conditional apex. Focus or input-context invalidation, death, or a weapon change cancels aim without firing. Melee attacks retain press-triggered, animation-driven hit timing and sensor lifetime. During player-controlled melee, directional input moves the player at full normal speed without changing the committed attack facing or hitbox orientation. Recovery cancellation follows the cooldown and physics-step rules above. A ready replacement melee attack uses the held direction, including when it replaces bow recovery. Bow release keeps its committed pose, and NPC and enemy attacks retain their committed facing. Validate authored arrow flight and tip data at loading, and validate sprite geometry against the loaded atlas when an arrow is drawn. Validate other authored data at its loading boundary and animation and geometry constraints where data meets loaded assets.

## Constraints
Existing `NPC` and `ENEMY` points on `test_tiled` spawn map-owned actors. Tiled `NpcSpawn` and `EnemySpawn` points prefer `spawnKey`, falling back to legacy `actorId` when no explicit key is authored; local keys are `basic_npc` and `brainless_undead` respectively. Mandatory `data/characters.json` and `data/enemies.json` are each top-level maps keyed by spawn key and may be empty. Spawn role selects the corresponding map. Each definition requires a nonblank `name` and positive integer `max_health`, which supplies initial and maximum spawn health. NPCs retain their loadout; enemies require a nullable top-level `weapon` instead. The player schema is unchanged. Animation `atlas_key` selects the appropriate sprite prefix. Both definitions author move speed, sounds, logical size, collision and hitbox bounds, `detection_bounds: { "radius": ... }` in pixels, and a required `behavior_graph` key. Graphs are reusable across definitions; sample keys are `wander` and `wander_attack`. The old `decision_graph` field is not accepted and has no alias. Migrate that field in the ignored raw actor files, preserve its values, and regenerate runtime assets. Missing or invalid required data fails loading, with no `actors.json` fallback. The locally ignored raw and runtime copies are migrated; other checkouts must migrate their own ignored data and regenerate runtime assets, preserving graph keys and detection radii.

Both the basic NPC and demo enemy have a dynamic body with a solid feet collision ellipse and a separate sensor hurtbox on the centered logical transform. Their independently authored local bounds match the player's 24×10 feet and 32×50 hurtbox; each defaults to 50 maximum health and can take damage. Their query-only detection circles are centered on their physical feet; the player is detectable but owns no circle. The required positive finite local NPC and enemy radii are 96 and 160 pixels, not code defaults. NPCs and enemies alternate wandering and pausing, with each wander and pause duration independently chosen from 1–3 seconds per actor. NPCs do not acquire targets by default. A per-actor Wander/Engage/Investigate FSM uses explicit Java transitions and selects the behavior graph for its current mode. An ordinary enemy acquires a living player within the forward semicircle of its feet-centered detection circle and unobstructed feet-to-feet line of sight. Cardinal facing defines the inclusive forward half-plane, including both side edges. Solid non-actor fixtures block sight; actors and sensors do not. Engage follows the detectable player at authored move speed, faces along the dominant axis, and records the player's last seen world-space feet coordinates. On loss of detection, Investigate steers directly to that point and returns to Wander at at most 8 logical pixels or after 2 seconds, whichever comes first. A detectable player can be reacquired on any tick. Only live sight can request melee. At up to 32 logical pixels, a ready actor may step toward the target until within 24 pixels while its decision is pending; during cooldown it plants its feet and faces the target. Each eligible default/base AI melee attack opportunity samples a 0.025–0.100 second decision delay once, reset when eligibility, target, or weapon changes. AI melee has at least 0.28 seconds from commitment to hit, followed by a planted recovery of at least 0.16 seconds after animation, with at least 0.4 seconds of authored follow-through plus recovery after the hit. The damage sensor does not persist into recovery. Perception updates during an attack, but melee motion and facing remain committed through the attack and recovery. Existing combat handles cooldown and damage. When a living NPC or enemy is damaged by a bow, it checks the current position of the living shooter at impact. It can identify and pursue that shooter with unobstructed feet-to-feet sight at up to three times the victim's detection radius; solid cover blocks sight. Retaliation ignores facing; enemies with spawn key `dredge` and NPCs also use full-circle retaliation when supported. Confirmed nonlethal player melee damage likewise provokes ordinary enemies, using the same three-radius range and solid-cover sight checks. NPC melee behavior is unchanged. An unarmed NPC can retaliate. Attacks require live sight and the existing attack cooldown; on sight loss, the actor uses the existing Investigate behavior. This retaliation does not give NPCs default aggression. An enemy with spawn key `dredge` detects a living player throughout its full detection circle with clear feet-to-feet sight, regardless of where it spawns. The Hoard Dredge guard also pursues regardless of health-area boundaries. On loss of sight or range it normally forgets the target and returns directly to its spawn, waits there, and can reacquire the player on the way; it does not investigate. A damaging player arrow provokes pursuit beyond normal sight and range until solid body contact or the player is no longer live, then normal detection and combat rules resume. Dredge's authored base is 300 HP; Hoard spawns add a flat 5 HP per round number, for 325 HP in round 5 and 350 in round 10. Committed attacks and windups remain planted on sight loss. In the physics debug overlay, an ordinary enemy's detection circle is replaced by its forward semicircle; the overlay does not visualize occlusion, and other circles remain unchanged. This adds no pathfinding or saved data. Neither actor has dialogue, player control, or camera ownership. Named travel rebuilds them with the map. Both raw and generated asset trees are local and ignored; provision the raw tree and run `./generate-runtime-assets.sh` before testing. Inspect the actors at the marked locations and travel away and back to verify rebuilding.

### Opt-in archers
An enemy definition can opt into ranged behavior by setting `behavior_graph` to `wander_archer` and `weapon` to `basic_bow` in the existing `enemies.json` map, then using its spawn key on an `ENEMY` spawn point. Existing enemy definitions and spawn points are not changed automatically. The archer profile retains wandering and investigation. With live sight and a primary projectile weapon, it holds position within half its configured detection radius and otherwise approaches between shots. Each eligible default/base AI archer attack opportunity samples a 0.15–0.30 second decision delay once, reset when eligibility, target, or weapon changes. Once the delay expires, it stops to draw and release, even if the target is farther away. It can reposition during projectile recovery while engaged; existing cooldown and live-sight requirements remain unchanged. With a non-projectile weapon it uses ordinary pursuit and melee behavior.

The archer draws to full draw and releases only while live sight continues to request the attack. Losing sight cancels the draw without firing or starting cooldown. Halfway through the draw, it locks facing and snapshots a predicted target center using the target's current velocity and the remaining draw plus curved-flight travel time; if the target cannot be intercepted within projectile lifetime, it aims directly at the current center. The locked aim is not updated before release. Prediction assumes constant velocity and does not guarantee a hit, particularly when the target changes direction or cover intervenes. The ordinary arrow collision and cooldown rules apply. AI aim does not display the player crosshair.

### Death and respawn
Every player, NPC, and enemy plays its death animation. Its final pose remains visible for five simulation seconds after playback finishes, then the corpse disappears. On ordinary maps, after the player's corpse disappears, the player respawns at full health at the current map's `PLAYER` point, falling back to the named arrival point if no `PLAYER` point exists. In `hoard_mode`, player death is terminal for that arena visit: do not respawn the player. The player retains the loadout and receives fresh combat and input state; the map and game time are not reset.

There is no magic requirement. `cast` is not a runtime dependency and is absent from the corrected raw inventory.

## Decisions
Compose visible characters into distinct offscreen cells. Within each cell, draw back to front: UP uses weapon, base, feet, body, hands, head; DOWN, LEFT, and RIGHT use base, feet, body, hands, head, weapon. Omit the weapon when unarmed. Batch atlas layers across characters by their actual texture page while preserving that order. Render each completed character as one sprite, world-depth sorted with ordinary objects; do not independently world-sort its weapon or other layers. Draw arrows after the map background and before depth-sorted world entities, including the player. The crosshair remains drawn after foreground map layers. This correction changes neither weapon placement in the order nor canvas, logical geometry, or assets.

Use correct premultiplied-alpha handling so transparent edges remain correct during both composition and final rendering.
